Biography

Dr. Xianglin Huang is a Visiting Research Professor at Dongguk University–Seoul and a Postdoctoral Researcher at the InnoCORE PRISM-AI Center, Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST). He received his Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Manchester (2024), supervised by Prof. Qingming Li.

His doctoral work developed a data-driven method for determining the dynamic flow stress of metals directly from discrete test data, using SVD/CP tensor decomposition and neural networks to test, rather than assume, the decoupled form behind the Johnson–Cook equation. Four first-authored papers in the International Journal of Impact Engineering (2023–2026) take the method from its mathematical basis to extrapolation at very high strain rates (TEDI-FS). The method is scoped to isotropic metals under J2 plasticity; other material classes would need a different formulation.

At KAIST he leads an independent research stream on lithium-ion battery impact safety: failure modes and energy thresholds under impact, real-time detection of latent damage from acoustic emission with deep learning, and multiphysics models linking mechanical damage to electrochemical degradation.

He has also published ten papers (2016–2026) in impact mechanics, on sandwich structures, layered plates, 3D woven composites, machine-learning surrogate models for impact resistance, and sheet-metal hardening. With Dr. Yunfei Deng he co-supervises master’s students, with work in progress on 3D-printed materials, lattice structures, and ceramics.
