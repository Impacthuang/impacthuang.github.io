Crash, impact, and perforation simulations all hinge on one question: how does a material flow at extreme strain rates? I develop data-driven methods that let experimental data, rather than assumed model forms, determine the flow stress of metals. Separately, I work on detecting latent impact damage in lithium-ion batteries.

Research Areas

Two Independent Research Streams

Data-driven constitutive modeling of metals and lithium-ion battery impact safety are pursued as independent programmes, each with its own questions, methods, and validation. They share access to impact-testing facilities, not assumptions.

Core Research
Data-Driven Constitutive Modeling
A crash or impact simulation is only as good as its flow stress model. I determine that model from discrete dynamic test data, using SVD/CP tensor decomposition to test whether a decoupled (Johnson–Cook-type) form is admissible and to extrapolate beyond the calibrated strain-rate range (TEDI-FS). Scope: isotropic metals under J2 plasticity.

Battery Impact Safety & Health Management
Lithium-ion cells can carry latent impact damage that shows no external sign. This stream characterizes failure modes and energy thresholds under impact, detects damage in real time from acoustic emission with deep learning, and links mechanical damage to electrochemical degradation with multiphysics FEM.

Why constitutive modeling? Every explicit finite element simulation of a crash, impact, or perforation event, whether in ABAQUS, LS-DYNA, or any other solver, needs a material model that tells the software how stress evolves with strain, strain rate, and temperature. The fidelity of the entire simulation is capped by the fidelity of that model. Making these models rigorous, data-driven, and validly extrapolable for metals is the core of this research; constitutive modeling of softer, more complex material classes is a separate, longer-term interest, not a current extension of this method.
